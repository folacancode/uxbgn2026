How this works

Three rules:

  1. Make more than you watch. If a week was all videos and no Figma file, that week didn't count.
  2. Design and build, even if it's ugly, then fix it. Your first screens will look bad. Everyone's did. Finish them anyway.
  3. Post your work every Sunday. On Twitter or LinkedIn. Every post builds your proof of work.

The Sunday check-in (you run it yourself)

No one is checking on you, so you check on yourself. Every Sunday, 30 minutes:

  1. Self-check. Answer that week's "Sunday check" questions honestly. A "no" means you repeat the task, not move on.
  2. AI critique. Screenshot your work and paste it into Claude or ChatGPT with the prompt below. Treat the answer as a second opinion, not a verdict.
  3. Post it. One post: what you made, what you learned, what's still bad.

Self-check prompt for step 2, AI critique. Copy it, fill in the brackets, and paste it with your screenshot:

You're a senior product designer in Lagos reviewing a beginner's work.
This is my [screen or flow] for [what it does], for [who uses it].
Give me the 3 biggest problems, ranked, with one specific fix for each.
Be blunt. Skip anything that's already fine.

Twice in the 8 weeks, after Week 4 and Week 8, book a free 30-minute ADPList session with a product designer in Lagos. Bring specific questions, not "how do I get a job."

After Week 8

One case study gets you in the door. This is the usual Nigerian route from here:

  1. Druids mentorship. Join the waitlist for the next cohort. It teaches building with AI, finding and pricing clients, and interviews, and it assumes you bring designs you've already made. You will have them.
  2. Second project. Pick a different kind of problem, for example a tool for a small business instead of a fintech flow. Two strong case studies are enough to apply for internships and junior roles.
  3. HNG Internship. It's free and team-based, with a product design track. HNG says plainly it's not for beginners, which is why it comes after this plan. Watch their site for the next cohort.
  4. First paid work. Businesses around you need help: the Instagram vendor with a messy order process, your church or school website, a friend's restaurant menu. Small paid jobs become portfolio pieces.
